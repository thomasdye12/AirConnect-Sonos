Coordinator role and concurrent native receivers — 14 October 2026

Latest result
A promoted receiver retained UDP/timing for a full 120-second test after the
helper was released. The previous roughly 76-second orphan-coordinator shutdown
did not occur in this case. However, Sonos reported PAUSED_PLAYBACK, not PLAYING.
This is evidence of a persistent receiver endpoint, NOT verified audible playback.
Initial setup still briefly uses another real stopped Sonos speaker.

Active-receiver promotion experiments
1. Server Room joins stopped Utility Room and starts ordinary-group silent UDP.
2. After three seconds, BecomeGroupCoordinator with x-sonos-clone-gc: 1 requests
   the receiver's own pre-test group ID, no other members, a synthetic line-in URI
   and an empty transport source. The command is accepted; topology names the
   real receiver itself as coordinator. Playback state changes to PAUSED_PLAYBACK.
3. RemoveMember on the helper removes the receiver, then helper
   BecomeCoordinatorOfStandaloneGroup releases its old group.
4. The same C sender and timing endpoint continue; no Play command is issued.
The 15-second test sent 2,635 packets and received 30 timing requests. The longer
120-second test sent 21,083 packets, received 135 timing requests and counted one
late send. Both devices ended in separate standalone groups. The long receiver
also survived a brief reuse of the helper for a second receiver experiment.
No Connect, AudioIn, VirtualLineIn, enrollment or security-policy calls were made.

Corrections and negative controls
- The first promotion used the inherited helper group ID and immediately reset
  the helper. It failed with ECONNREFUSED after 628 packets and seven timing
  requests. That sequence did not isolate promotion from helper cleanup.
- Repeating promotion without resetting the helper kept UDP/timing alive for
  15 seconds, although state was PAUSED_PLAYBACK. Device topology views disagreed
  about the old group's coordinator. Promotion alone did not close the sink.
- Using the receiver's own group ID and removing stale membership before helper
  reset preserved the endpoints in the 15/120-second tests described above.
- Adding Play after detached promotion returned success but ended the native
  stream: ECONNREFUSED after 690 packets/eight clock requests. The failure
  snapshot was STOPPED with its prior source URI. Play acceptance is not proof
  of native source activation or retained playback.
- Kitchen accepted promotion but the helper's subsequent RemoveMember returned
  800. That test aborted and restored both devices. It does not prove two
  concurrent detached sessions; the observer ran after Kitchen was restored.
  Server Room's long test continued through the helper reuse and cleanup.
- The promoted receiver tests deliberately fail the harness playback check when
  state is PAUSED_PLAYBACK, even if all planned packets and clocks complete.

Concurrent PLAYING control with retained coordinator
A separate centralized test managed two receivers with stopped Utility Room as
coordinator. Both joined the same ordinary group and received separate unicast
zero-PCM streams from distinct C senders and timing ports.
- Server Room: 90 seconds, 15,812 packets, 120 timing requests, zero late sends.
- Kitchen: 150 seconds, 26,354 packets, 151 timing requests, one late send.
- Both reported PLAYING/OK during overlap. Server Room was PLAYING/OK just before
  detachment, restoration to its previous source, and Stop at 90 seconds.
- Kitchen stayed PLAYING/OK immediately after that stop and at the end; its
  packet count continued increasing. The helper remained STOPPED/OK.
This verifies bounded concurrent receiver control and independent stopping with
one retained physical coordinator. Both streams contained silence. Different
music content, audible continuity and separate AirPlay integration are unverified.
A late-send counter here means the sender was over six milliseconds past its
scheduled packet deadline; it is not a measured speaker dropout.

Static explanation — nearby Play:3 86.10-81100, installed 86.10-80260
- monitorCoordinator's self-coordinator branch at 0x1034a048..0x1034a060 clears
  the failure counter and returns zero; its wrapper then stops periodic checks.
  This is consistent with the detached endpoint lasting past the old timeout.
  It is not an installed-build stack trace or proof of acoustic rendering.
- Peer-group lookup at 0x10694f0c first uses eligible lookup 0x1069e1c0, then
  0x1069b91c requires peer flags +0x1f18 and +0x1f4b and calls 0x10699620.
  Only then is group ID +0xde5 copied and compared. Matching an advertised
  group ID alone does not establish eligibility for a synthetic coordinator.
- The transport parser 0x105c661c requires its tokens, including the final source.
  An empty source can leave default settings. The successful promotion need not
  install the sender endpoints in its request; the prior active sink may persist.
- BecomeGroupCoordinatorAndSource at 0x102bc848 parses CurrentSourceState through
  0x102f0bac (call at 0x102bcc78). It expects 164 characters: 100 characters of
  15 fixed-width hexadecimal fields and a 64-character source-format string.
  Success requires 15 conversions and format parsing. Serializer 0x102f0968
  uses the same format at 0x10c132d4. Field meanings are not all reconstructed.
  This is not simply a sender IP/UDP-port string; no usable external-source
  activation through this alternate method has been established.
- The exposed /status index and /support/review did not provide coordinator-loss
  logs. Diagnostics did not confirm the inferred monitor by a runtime trace.

Code, restoration and next gate
multi_receiver_probe.py owns all three devices for the retained-coordinator test,
so stopping one does not restore the shared helper beneath the other. It requires
stopped standalone devices, allocates both senders before mutation, caps duration
at 180 seconds, sends silence only and verifies restoration.
group_receiver_probe.py --promote-receiver --promotion-mode detach tests the
promoted state; mode retain keeps the old group, and resume additionally calls
Play. These are experimental sequences, not a deployable session manager.
Eleven offline checks passed, including allocation failure, refusal, independent
rollback attempts, and preventing packet/clock progress from becoming a false
playback-success result. All seven live cases verified original source/metadata,
transport, mute, volume and standalone grouping restored, with no restore errors.
Production AirConnect was not changed or restarted. No firmware or pairing work.

Next useful gate: determine whether the detached PAUSED_PLAYBACK state actually
renders incoming PCM, through a controlled listening test when available. Then
resolve helper-detachment portability and the remaining bootstrap dependency.
Do not claim independent audible playback, measured latency or an integrated
AirPlay backend from these silent results.
